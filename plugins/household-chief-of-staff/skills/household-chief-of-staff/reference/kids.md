# Kids' Recurring Logistics

A checklist of things that come back every year for young kids. Fill in the family's actual dates in `tasks.md` and the calendar. Follow the pediatrician over this list.

## Health

- **Well-child visits:** the AAP Bright Futures schedule has a well visit every year from age 3 (more often before that). Book them around birthdays or the start of the school year so they're easy to remember. https://www.aap.org/periodicity-schedule
- **Immunizations:** follow the CDC schedule and the pediatrician. The CDC recommends a yearly flu vaccine for everyone 6 months and older. Put it on the calendar every fall. https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/
- **Dental:** cleanings on the schedule the family dentist recommends.
- **School health forms:** physicals, immunization records, and medication authorizations are often due before the school year starts. Check the school's deadlines.

## School-year cycle (adjust to the family's district)

- **Summer:** registration, supply lists, camp forms, physicals.
- **Start of year:** emergency contact forms, lunch accounts, back-to-school night, picture day.
- **Fall:** parent-teacher conferences, Halloween costume and parade, book fair, flu shots.
- **Winter:** holiday breaks, teacher gifts, winter gear (sizes change every year).
- **Spring:** next-year registration, spring break, camp sign-ups (often fill up early), end-of-year gifts.
- **Every week:** spirit days, library day, folder and homework, lunch money, early release days.

If a kids' school/activities scanning skill is installed, use it to find these items in email and Drive.

## Birthdays and social

- Track each child's birthday and plan about 6 weeks ahead (venue, invitations).
- Keep a short list of gift ideas for classmates' parties so a "yes" doesn't turn into a last-minute store run.

## One-on-one time

With three kids, each child gets less individual attention by default. Put a short, regular one-on-one slot for each child on a rotation in `routines.md`. *(Practical judgment.)*
